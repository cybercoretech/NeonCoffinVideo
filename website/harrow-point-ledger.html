HARBOR ROAD / THE UNDERTOW OFFICE / AFTER MIDNIGHT

The press runs on Saturday night.

Ephraim Pike sets every line by hand on the same iron press the paper has used since 1821. The Sunday edition is on the doorsteps before dawn. Some weeks it arrives before the news does.

The Harrow Point Undertow

Weather ForecastFog until the road is gone.Clearing inland.
VOL. CCVI — No. 10,671SUNDAY, OCTOBER 18, 2026PRICE ONE PENNY

Established 1821 · Printed weekly by E. Pike upon the hand press · What the tide takes, the Undertow records.

Harbor Bell Heard Three Times Below Water

Buoy found chained above the tide line — Captain Rook declines comment — Bosun will not leave the pier

Residents along the eastern quay reported the harbor bell shortly after midnight on Thursday, sounding three times from somewhere below the water. The bell buoy itself was found the following morning chained to its iron ring on the quay, a full six feet above the tide line, where it has rested since the Drowned Bell Gale of 1996.

Captain Elias Rook, who keeps the slip nearest the buoy, declined to comment. His dog, Bosun, was observed at the end of the pier until dawn and could not be persuaded inside. Neither has been seen to sleep since.

Constable Wainwright asks residents to keep to the upper road after sundown and to leave their lamps lit on the harbor side. The notice reached this office before the first call reached his. Fishermen are advised that the tide is out of season and should not be relied upon for the remainder of the month.

Older readers will recall the bell was last heard below water in the week the Annie Thorne failed to return. The Undertow’s file for that week contains a full account. It is not yet ready to be printed.

Mayor’s Address

From the office of the Mayor

The Town Hall wishes all residents a quiet Sunday. The October Town Festival will proceed as it always has. Residents are thanked for their continued attendance, which has never once been in doubt.

The Mayor reminds the town that Harrow Point has no need of a census. The population stands at one hundred eighty-two and will be kept there.

— By Order, The Mayor

Council Approves Motion

Old coast road to remain open — Map to be left as found

The council voted without objection to keep the coast road open past Vesper House. The town map in the hall will not be corrected. Members noted that it has corrected itself before.

Shipping News

Arrived: none scheduled. One.
Departed: none.
Overdue: the Annie Thorne, forty-one years.

Local & Otherwise

Notices From the Point

Pier Lights After Closing

The Amusement Pier will remain closed until its lamps stop moving. Music was heard from the carousel after the power was cut. No operator has been scheduled. The organ that burned in 1979 has not been found, though it has been heard.

Traveler Not Accounted For

A motorist last seen asking directions at the coast road has not returned to the county line. Their name was withheld at the family’s request. The Undertow has left space for it.

 

October Festival Reminder

Residents are asked not to follow the midway beyond the last lit booth. Parents are reminded that no children are registered in town this year, nor in any year on file.

Monthly Column

In the Margins

The type was set before the words

One Sunday each month, the last column arrives on the composing stone with no copy attached. Mr. Pike says the type is already warm.

“If the paper prints your name, do not ask who set it. If it does not, do not ask why.”

A correction to last week’s edition: the woman seen walking the coast road on Tuesday was not Miss Vera Wren. Miss Wren’s shadow was seen walking the coast road on Tuesday. The Undertow regrets the error.

A correction to next week’s edition will follow.

— From the editor’s desk, unsigned

Weather

From the Morgue Files / 1821 – Present

Obituaries

Notices of the departed, as printed in these pages — The Undertow does not print retractions

A Note to Newcomers

In Harrow Point an obituary is a notice of a change in status, not of absence. A death is recorded, a service is held, and a stone is cut and dated on the north wall at Widow’s Rise. The ground beneath it is not disturbed.

It is the custom of the town that the deceased attend. It is considered poor manners to remark upon it, and worse manners to ask where they intend to go afterward.

— E. Pike, for the Undertow

Services This Week

Tuesday, ten o’clock. Mrs. Margery Pell, at Low Tide Mortuary. The family will receive mourners. So will Mrs. Pell.

Thursday, at low water. A service for a name not yet supplied. Mourners are asked to come anyway.

April 1821

Josiah Crane

Cooper, aged about sixty. Died at his bench of a seizure of the heart. Funeral held at Widow’s Rise, the first service recorded there. Mr. Crane attended and was observed to weep at the appropriate moment. He resumed his trade on the Monday following.

Status: At his bench

November 1847

Mrs. Hester Lowell

Keeper of the Point light. Lost from the gallery of the tower during the gale of that month. A service was read without the body. Mrs. Lowell lit the lamp that evening at the usual hour, and every evening since.

Status: On duty

October 1918

Eleven Residents

No deaths from the influenza were recorded in Harrow Point. The Undertow printed eleven obituaries as a precaution. Services were held together at Low Tide Mortuary. All eleven attended and pronounced the lilies excessive.

Status: Attending, all eleven

February 1931

Dr. Cornelius Abbot

Physician to the town for forty years. Pronounced himself dead at ten minutes past four in the morning and signed the certificate in his own hand. He keeps no office and still makes his house calls on the usual rounds. Patients are asked not to mention it during examinations.

Status: On his rounds

August 1952

Mrs. Ruth Ellery

Ticket-seller at the Amusement Pier, aged seventy-one. Services at Low Tide Mortuary. Mrs. Ellery sat in the front row and was heard to say the casket was cheap. Her booth reopened Saturday. The prices have not changed.

Status: Selling tickets

September 1979

The Pier Ballroom Orchestra

Nine musicians, lost in the ballroom fire on Labor Day night. Services private. None of the nine attended. The carousel organ was not recovered. The orchestra has asked, through channels this paper will not name, that no further notices be printed.

Status: Not returned

March 1985

The Crew of the Annie Thorne

Seven men, overdue from the eastern grounds. A memorial was read on the quay. No one stood at the back. Families are asked to leave the harbor-side lamps lit until the vessel is accounted for.

Status: Overdue

June 2003

Walter “Dutch” Haskell

Lobsterman, aged fifty-eight. Taken by the cold water off the Point. Mr. Haskell delivered his own eulogy, which was well received. Mourners noted he had not prepared it, and that it seemed to be about someone else.

Status: Hauling traps

This Week

Mrs. Margery Pell

Of Cove Lane, aged eighty-eight. Died peacefully on Sunday. Services Tuesday at Low Tide Mortuary, with burial to follow at Widow’s Rise. In the meantime Mrs. Pell will receive visitors at home. Kindly bring a dish.

Status: Receiving visitors

THE EDITOR / PRESSMAN

Ephraim Pike

Pike runs the old hand press and has no known predecessor. The paper’s surviving archive lists him as pressman in years before his birth. He says the first issue was already waiting when he found the print room, and insists he only sets the type he finds.

His Down East voice is dry and spare; he speaks in short proofs, correcting himself only when the type is wrong. Each Sunday he prints the town notices. On the final Sunday of each month, the column called “In the Margins” appears without his byline.
